The working-set limit

Human
Working memory: 7±2 items.
Comprehension is limited. Nobody truly reviews a 50-page design document.
LLM
Limited attention focus.
Ask one agent to focus on 100 things and the quality per perspective drops significantly. IFScale benchmark [1]

Try it: review with 1 agent and 10 perspectives, then with a subagent per perspective. The second is far more comprehensive.

[1] Jaroslawicz et al., How Many Instructions Can LLMs Follow at Once? (IFScale benchmark), 2025: even frontier models drop to ~68% adherence at 500 simultaneous instructions.

What the physical breakdown added

The structure itself IS information.

The pieces are not just the same text: a knowledge graph with metadata and purpose-dependent traversals.

Small, focused contexts for the LLM.

An LLM works far better on a bounded slice than on one giant context.

Change becomes traceable.

E.g. change an invariant and you see exactly what depends on it.

Refactorable structure, real CI/CD on the design model.

The structure ITSELF can be iterated: enables innovation.

The objection, said out loud

“Better models will fix it”

Compensational structures

Work around current LLM behaviour. These will age out, so don’t over-invest here.

Information-carrying structures

Clarification, intent delivery, decisions and assumptions. Better models cannot infer the underdetermined, and will leverage this space even more effectively.

Rule of thumb: build the structure that encodes decisions and intent; skip what merely works around mechanics.

The irreducible remainder

  • Thought experiment: a perfect agent edits the corpus flawlessly and keeps it fully coherent. What still remains?
  • Entitlement: some decisions have to be made by a human in a given context.
  • Comprehension: the human must see what they are saying yes to.
  • Problem emergence: information created by the process cannot be inferred up front, not even by a perfect model.
Structure is the human control interface.

Applied

Claims → agent autonomy

  • Every decision the agent makes, every claim, is classified: decided in the model / derived from it / a NEW decision.
  • The loop stops exactly where a new semantic decision is needed; everything else is mechanical.
  • Autonomy is proportional to contract density: hands-off exactly where acceptance is machine-checkable.
  • Human attention goes only where it is genuinely needed.
Applied

Designing LLM review loops

Without triage…

…a neverending nitpicking loop. One project’s first cycle: ~100 rounds, ~700 findings, zero rejections. Dispositions are not optional.

Design the stop

LLMs are better at finding something than at being done. Mechanical stopping rule + altitude discipline.

Lenses, not redundancy

One perspective per agent; distinct lenses (falsifier / domain plausibility / fresh reader); an external model family breaks self-reference.

The fresh reader is a forecast

What a fresh reviewer misreads, the fresh implementer will misread too. Reviews measure intent transfer.

Designing the lenses: TO EXPAND

Draft: the real depth is in HOW a lens is designed. Examples to bring: mutation-style tests (seed a known defect; does the lens catch it?); evidence obligations (the reviewer must PROVE a claim with running code, not just assert it).

Everyone optimizes for quality, nobody for value. Build the value counterweight in (850 lines of spec for 10 lines of code).
Applied

Designing LLM guidance

  • A principle is worth only as much as it is operable.
  • What makes it stick: a trigger (say WHEN) · question-form over statement · red flags (name the symptoms) · one canonical example.
  • Keep it narrow: 4–5 triggered principles beat 15. The checklist is the ceiling, not the floor.
  • Proof: a rule kept failing, until a mandatory question at the moment of action.
  • Guidance is itself a tiny design corpus; the same laws apply.
Takeaways

Five things to take home

  • Structure creates information: the corpus is a system of relationships; the breakdown itself creates new, leverageable information.
  • It serves both minds. Humans and LLMs get the same thing: understanding and navigation of the design space.
  • The design can breathe, and can be verified: structure makes change safe and the result checkable.
  • Route human attention by decision type. The agent runs free on decided and derived; the human handles the genuinely new.
  • Design for the LLM’s characteristics, especially the feedback loops: without triage and a stop rule, a neverending nitpicking loop.
