The working-set limit

Human
Working memory: 7±2 items.
Comprehension is limited.
Nobody truly reviews a 50-page design document.
LLM
Limited attention focus.
Ask one agent to focus on 100 things and the quality per perspective drops significantly. IFScale benchmark [1]

Try it: review with 1 agent and 10 perspectives, then with a subagent per perspective.

The second is far more comprehensive.

[1] Jaroslawicz et al., How Many Instructions Can LLMs Follow at Once? (IFScale benchmark), 2025: even frontier models drop to ~68% adherence at 500 simultaneous instructions.

LLM traits behind the pain

LLMs are non-deterministic.

Super capable at finding issues in context, but usually not all of them.

LLMs have a limited focus: they find issues better in a focused context.

Designing LLM review loops

The fresh reader is a forecast

What a fresh reviewer misreads, the fresh implementer will misread too.

Reviews measure intent transfer.

Without triage: a neverending nitpicking loop

War story

One project’s first approach: ~100 rounds, ~700 findings, zero rejections.

Dispositions are not optional.

Design the stop

LLMs are better at finding something than at being done.

Mechanical stopping rule + altitude discipline.

An LLM is trained to finish the task, not to ask whether the change
is worth making. The loop must carry the value judgment.

seen in practice 850 lines of spec proposed for 10 lines of code.

More compute, better results

Designing the lenses

the roster

Lenses, not redundancy

One perspective per agent. Three agents reading the same way is one review paid for three times.

An external model family is a lens too, and the only one that breaks self-reference.
mutation-style

Validate the lens itself

Seed a known defect into the corpus, then run the lens. Does it catch it?

An unvalidated lens produces confident silence.
Monitor lens yield too: sometimes you retire a lens, because the structure or the guidance improved.
the obligation

Evidence obligations

The reviewer must attach evidence to every claim, not just assert it.

LLM reviewers are non-deterministic: sometimes the claim is simply wrong. Evidence makes it re-checkable.

A lens is an artifact: design it, validate it, version it.

Takeaways

Five things to take home

  1. 1

    Structure creates informationThe corpus is a system of relationships;
    the breakdown itself creates new, leverageable information.

  2. 2

    It serves both mindsHumans and LLMs get the same thing:
    understanding and navigation of the design space.

  3. 3

    The design can breathe, and can be verifiedStructure makes change safe and the result checkable.

  4. 4

    Route human attention by decision typeThe agent runs free on decided and derived;
    the human handles the genuinely new.

  5. 5

    Design for the LLM’s characteristicsEspecially the feedback loops:
    without triage and a stop rule, a neverending nitpicking loop.
